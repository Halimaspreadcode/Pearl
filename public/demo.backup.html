01 The floor

It starts with a screen someone is watching

A supervision centre runs on attention. Somebody is looking at alarms right now, and the first question they need answered is never complicated: is anything slipping.

The dashboard answers that in one screen. Volumes, how much of the backlog is still inside its deadline, who is carrying the load, and what has already breached. Nothing here needs a click to be understood, which matters when it is read twenty times a day.

One control shapes everything else: the Global, SN and CI switch. A country lead selects their perimeter and the entire product narrows to it, sidebar included. A regional lead stays on Global and reads both. Same product, two jobs.

02 The ticket

Something is wrong, and the clock starts

An alarm becomes a ticket. From that second a deadline runs, and the whole point of the tracker is that nobody has to remember it.

The clock starts at the occurrence time, not the moment someone typed it in. That single decision is what keeps the measurement honest: an incident found late is still an incident that started early, and a tracker that pretended otherwise would flatter the team.

Critical anomalies carry two deadlines rather than one. Restoring service and fixing the root cause are different promises, so they are timed separately. A workaround that holds does not close the underlying problem, and the product refuses to conflate them.

Not everything worth remembering is a ticket.

A recurring smell on a link, a customer who called twice, a workaround that should not become permanent. None of these justify opening an incident, and all of them matter to whoever takes the next shift.

Observations exist for exactly that. Free of the ticket lifecycle, so nobody has to invent a fake incident just to write something down.

03 The shift

Someone has to be there at three in the morning

Behind every ticket is a rota that put a person in a chair. Planning is where the operation is really decided, and where most of the friction lives.

The month is pre built from the configured sites and slots, then adjusted by hand, because reality always differs. What matters is what happens next: an agent asks for a change, and that request stops being a message in a group chat. It enters a queue, gets a decision, and leaves a trace.

The planner also reads the absence calendar. If somebody is booked on a shift while declared away, the conflict surfaces on its own. Only future conflicts are shown, since a shift that already happened cannot be fixed and listing it only buries the ones that still can.

A request stops being a message in a group chat.

The agent submits from their own screen, the decision is taken in administration, and the person asking always sees where their request stands. That visibility is what removes the follow up messages, not the approval workflow itself.

04 When someone is away

Not all absences mean the same thing

A holiday, a fever and a training day all remove someone from the floor, but treating them alike would be both unfair and wrong.

Eleven leave types are defined, each with three properties: does it consume the annual balance, is it paid, can it be planned. Only paid leave and RTT eat into the balance. Sick leave never does, which is the behaviour anyone would expect and yet surprisingly easy to get wrong.

Days are counted in working days with the country holidays removed, and pending requests are already reserved. A balance that looks available but is about to be consumed would be worse than no balance at all.

An unjustified absence costs 25 points on the attendance criterion, enough on its own to drop below the 85 percent team threshold. The rule is written down rather than left to judgement, so it is the same rule for everyone.

05 Getting better

Learning that actually counts for something

Training tends to be the first thing announced and the first thing dropped. It survives here because it feeds the rating, and because the way it is scored rewards the behaviour you actually want.

The naive approach is to take the best quiz score and stop there. That rewards one good afternoon and ignores everything else. So the score combines four things: how well courses are passed, how many different courses are covered, how consistent the effort is over time, and the level reached.

The practical effect is simple. Retaking the same quiz ten times changes nothing. Passing ten different courses with a steady rhythm takes you to the top of the scale. Effort keeps paying all the way to 100.

The streak is the number of consecutive days with activity.

It is the one figure that separates a steady learner from a single good afternoon, and it is the reason consistency carries a fifth of the score rather than nothing at all.

Experience, level and streak are recomputed on the server at every attempt, so none of it can be adjusted from the browser.

06 The monthly rating

Six criteria, and where each number comes from

Everything so far converges here. Tickets, absences and training are not separate records, they are the evidence behind a monthly conversation.

Four of the six criteria are computed from data the product already holds. The manager can change any of them, and the manual entry always wins, because a month has context that no measurement captures. What the product guarantees is that the starting point is never invented.

A score is the weighted mean of the criteria that were actually filled in, renormalised. Grading four criteria out of six does not mechanically cap the result at 55, which would have punished the manager rather than described the agent.

07 The review

Sitting down with one person

A one to one that starts with opening five screens has already lost ten minutes and most of its attention. The agent profile exists to remove that.

One page gathers what the product knows: a radar over the six criteria, the goals with their milestones and due dates, the actions agreed last time, and the full interview history. The radar matters because a dent in a shape is spotted before the labels are read, which a column of six numbers never achieves.

Strengths and watch points are derived from the measurements with explicit thresholds. Nothing is asserted without data. A criterion that was never filled in appears in neither column, rather than being quietly counted as a weakness.

Actions agreed in an interview are extracted line by line and carried to the next one. An action that was never picked up shows as still open, which is usually the most useful thing on the page.

08 Telling the story

Somebody upstairs asks how the month went

The same records that ran the floor produce the reporting, which is the only way the numbers in a steering committee ever match the numbers on the operations screen.

Daily, weekly and monthly reports are generated from live data, and the weekly deck exports to PowerPoint server side with the team layout. That export is deliberately disabled outside the Senegal perimeter, because the template is a Senegal report and letting it run on CI would produce a document that contradicts its own title.

Two companion screens keep the data trustworthy. Data Quality flags tickets with missing fields, impossible dates or contradictory statuses. The Audit Trail records who changed what, when, and what the previous value was.

The numbers in a steering committee match the numbers on the floor.

Not because someone reconciled two spreadsheets, but because there is only one set of records. Ops Review sits alongside the weekly report for the deeper cut, breaking a period down by domain, priority and affiliate.

09 Behind the scenes

Everything above is configurable

None of the rules described here are hard coded. Deadlines, domains, statuses, team composition, rotation sites, quiz bank, interview template and rating targets all live in configuration.

Eleven settings areas sit behind a single unlock that lasts eight hours, reachable from one overview with a search field. The search matches both the title and the description, so looking for the word password finds Access and security without knowing it was called that.

Two areas deserve a mention. Live sessions shows who is connected and on which page, and allows a session to be closed remotely. Access and security is where personal codes are issued for the appraisal view described next.

10 Who sees what

Appraisal data stays where it belongs

A rating is a personal record. An interview note is a personal record. The product treats them that way, and the enforcement lives on the server rather than in the interface.

That distinction is the whole point. Hiding a screen is not protection if the data has already travelled to the browser, where anyone can read it from the console. The route refuses, not the interface.

An agent reads their own level with a Huawei ID and a personal code issued by their manager. The code is stored hashed and shown once, at creation. The endpoint takes no agent identifier at all, so there is no request shape that returns a colleague's score, even with a valid session.
